# Gypsum-Board Ceiling Detailing for Lighting

Dimensions below are practical starting points that work on site; confirm against
the manufacturer's system data and the project's structural/fire requirements.

## Board and system basics

- Standard board 12.5 mm for typical residential ceilings; 15 mm or double-layer
  where spans, curves or premium finish demand it.
- Board type by location: standard in dry rooms, moisture-resistant (MR/green)
  in bathrooms, kitchens and laundries, fire-rated (FR/Type X) where a rated
  ceiling assembly is required (garages, plant, boundary to another unit).
- Single-skin ceiling on a furring/hat-channel grid: the fixing goes to the
  framing, never to the board alone. Even where the architect wants a flat
  "no visible frame" edge, the channel must appear on the detail.
- Curved cove / ogee: multiple layers of 9.5–12.5 mm board, wet-formed or
  kerf-bent, with the curve radius stated and the strip's bend radius matched.
- Finish: skim or tape-and-fill per spec. Grazing light exposes every ridge, so
  a "level 4" finish under a cove is a visible defect — specify level 5 where
  the surface is washed.

## Indirect cove (the most common villa detail)

Working geometry, all dimensions finished face:

- Cove ledge/depth: 100–150 mm visible face; 120 mm is a safe default.
- Upturn height: 80–150 mm above the ledge so light reaches the ceiling plane.
- Strip offset from the upturn: 60–100 mm; wider is softer, but keep the strip
  out of the direct view line from the lowest seating position (check a 45° view
  line from eye height).
- Clearance around the strip: minimum 25 mm all round for heat; 50 mm if the
  strip sits in a closed pocket.
- Bounce surface: an indirect cove needs a light-coloured ceiling above it and
  roughly 150–250 mm of ceiling for the wash to read. A cove under a 40 mm slot
  lights nothing.
- Output: the cove is the ambient layer; assume roughly 60–70 % of the strip
  lumens land on the ceiling in a good detail, less in a shallow one. Model it
  in the calculation, don't assume 100 %.
- Joinery: plaster bead at the cove lip for a crisp shadow line; fix the strip
  to the structure, not to the bead.

## Recessed downlight / spotlight pockets

- Cut-out 75 mm (small), 90 mm (standard), 100–120 mm (large/trimless); cut-out,
  can depth and trim type must all match the schedule.
- Can depth clearance: measure the actual fixture including driver — 80–120 mm
  typical, but show it. Recess depth 120 mm is a comfortable default.
- Trimless (plaster-in) fixtures need a 2–3 mm reveal and a dedicated plaster
  frame; specify the frame, not "flush with ceiling".
- IC-rated (insulation-contact) required where the ceiling is insulated;
  otherwise show an air gap and a non-combustible barrier.
- Spacing: general uniform grid ≈ 1–1.2 × ceiling height to the floor; keep the
  perimeter row 600–900 mm off the wall (≈ half the spacing) and never light a
  wall from 200 mm out.
- Beam: 24–36° for accent, 50–60° for general washing.
- Fire hoods / acoustic hoods where the assembly is rated or where bedrooms sit
  over noisy rooms.

## Linear and shadow-gap details

- Linear recessed channel: 50 × 50 mm or 75 × 75 mm plaster-in channel, diffuser
  sitting 5–10 mm below the plaster face so dust does not land on it. Specify the
  diffuser (opal / microprismatic) — a bare strip in a channel reads as a hot line.
- Shadow gap: 10–15 mm gap, strip at the back, no visible LED. The reveal must be
  parallel and constant; call out a tolerance above ±2 mm.
- Curtain pocket: 120–150 mm wide × 100–120 mm deep minimum, with a downward
  strip inside if the client wants the curtain washed; keep the strip on the
  window side so it does not glare into the room.

## Loads, coordination and access

- Board carries ~0.5–1 kg per small downlight; anything above ~10 kg (large
  chandelier, ceiling fan, cluster pendant) must hang from framing, Unistrut or a
  backing plate shown on the detail. Never a blockboard screwed to board alone.
- Ceiling fan: dedicated box/framing plus the manufacturer's minimum drop.
- AC diffuser: keep general downlights ~500 mm clear of a linear slot diffuser and
  out of the throw, so warm air does not wash the beam; coordinate the AC layout
  before releasing the ceiling plan.
- Smoke detector: per NFPA 72 practice, at least 900 mm horizontally from an HVAC
  supply diffuser and 100 mm clear of any obstruction/edge; never buried between a
  downlight and a cove.
- Sprinkler heads: coordinate per SBC 801 / NFPA 13; keep fixtures from shadowing
  a head or breaking the required spray coverage.
- Access panels: every driver, transformer, control module and linear-strip joint
  must be reachable. If it cannot be reached, put it on a removable panel and say
  so on the detail.
- Ceiling void: aim for 150 mm clear for plain recessed lighting, 250–300 mm where
  drivers, linear channels and AC ducts share the void.

## KSA-specific site risks

- Dust: the first thing a Saudi villa ceiling shows is plaster dust in cove
  pockets. Specify a cleanable detail (continuous pocket with removable access) or
  accept the shadow line where dust will land.
- Thermal: LED drivers in a closed ceiling pocket at 45–50 °C ambient derate hard.
  Prefer remote drivers, or IC-rated fixtures with real airflow.
- Expansion and cracking: show control joints at long plaster runs; running a
  linear strip across a control joint guarantees a cracked shadow line.
