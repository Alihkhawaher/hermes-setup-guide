# Exterior / Facade / Landscape Lighting for Villas

## Layer map

1. **Facade** — grazing, wall-wash or a deliberate accent on one feature. The
   facade is a composition: light some planes, leave others dark. A uniformly
   floodlit villa reads as a car park.
2. **Entrance** — the brightest exterior point; also the security camera zone.
3. **Circulation** — paths, steps, level changes. Legibility beats beauty.
4. **Landscape** — trees, planting beds, boundary walls.
5. **Water** — pool and features (electrical rules below).
6. **Amenity** — terrace, pergola, seating, BBQ.
7. **Perimeter/security** — combined with CCTV coverage; lighting alone is not
   security, and over-lighting the fence line creates glare for the neighbours.

## Ingress protection and impact

| Location | Minimum |
|---|---|
| Open to rain, wall-mounted | IP65 |
| Ground/recessed, drainage exposure | IP66–IP67 |
| Permanently submerged (pool, fountain) | IP68, 12 V SELV |
| Covered porch, soffit | IP44 |
| Under-eave / carport with wind-blown rain | IP65 |
| Impact/abrasion exposure (sand) | IK08+ housing, powder-coated or anodised |

Coastal projects: 316 stainless or hot-dip/powder-coated aluminium; ordinary
zinc-plated fittings fail within a couple of seasons.

## Electrical safety (verify against SBC 401 / the local utility rules)

- Pool and fountain luminaires: extra-low voltage (SELV, typically 12 V) with an
  isolating transformer outside the pool zone, or the manufacturer's equivalent;
  equipotential bonding and 30 mA RCD protection must be shown on the electrical
  drawings.
- All exterior circuits on 30 mA RCD protection, separate from interior final
  circuits, with transformers/drivers accessible.
- Cable: exterior-rated (UV and water resistant) in conduit, gel-filled joints,
  glands at every enclosure entry. Exposed cable across a facade is a finding.
- KSA supply is 230 V / 60 Hz (confirm per project/utility); specify drivers for
  the actual mains, not 50 Hz-only stock.

## Glare, dark-sky and neighbour control

- Shield everything. If the lamp is visible from the street or an upstairs
  bedroom window, it is mis-specified.
- Uplights: aimed at the facade/object with a shield or hood; a bare uplight on a
  boundary wall throws light into the sky and into the neighbour's yard.
- In-ground uplights glare into a walking eye line — use a honeycomb or snoot, or
  move the fixture to the other side of the object.
- Aim the facade wash to leave deliberate unlit areas; 10:1–30:1 lit:unlit
  contrast is a useful target.

## Control

- Astronomic/solar time clock or a control system with seasonal adjustment, not a
  fixed 6 pm timer.
- Scenes: "arrival" (all on), "evening" (facade + amenity), "late/night" (paths +
  security only, dimmed to 20–30 %), "holiday/event".
- Motion sensors for driveway and side paths; a manual override at the entrance.
- Dim the night scene rather than switching everything off — a dark villa with
  three bright spots looks worse than a dim, layered one.

## KSA climate and maintenance

- Ambient 45–50 °C with fine dust: seal drivers in ventilated compartments, derate
  output, specify IP-rated connectors; a driver that dies in year two is usually
  heat or dust, not a bad brand.
- Sand-laden wind abrades polycarbonate lenses: prefer glass, or set a lens
  replacement interval.
- Drainage: in-ground fixtures need gravel/sand drainage underneath, or the first
  rain floods them.
- Cleaning access: assume the client will not clean anything; specify fixtures
  that read well when dusty (fewer, larger, shielded) rather than many small
  sparkling ones.
